# Community posts

A forum reply, an issue or a feature request goes out under the user's name. The regulars of a community know
each other's voices, and a post that reads like a model loses its hearing, most of all where people dislike AI.
Before you draft, read the recent posts of a few regulars in the same place (a profile's post list shows them), and
the user's own earlier posts there if any. Then write the way the experienced users write. Keep the English simple
when the user writes simple English.

Learn from the users who help and build, not from staff or from people who vent. Leave out:

- Support or company phrasing: "We'd be happy to help", "Apologies for the inconvenience", "Just a heads up"
- Extra politeness: a thank-you or an emoji in every reply, "sir", praise before the point
- Rudeness, sarcasm and venting: "Pls don't ...", "GG", "I'm begging you", "it's horrible"

What experienced users do:

- Put the answer or the request first, with no lead-in: "You can surround it with invisible solid borders"
- Name the exact behavior, property, menu path, error text or version, then stop
- Back a claim with their own case: "I tested it on my biggest projects", "I run into this from time to time too"
- Mark doubt in plain words where it exists: "I think", "probably", "as far as I can tell", "I haven't confirmed
  this"
- Offer another way in one line: "Or ...", "Another idea could be ..."
- Add a later finding as an "EDIT:" line instead of rewriting the post
- Put a side suggestion to staff in one low-key line: "btw Ashley, I think X should be considered"
- In a feature request, give the parts uneven weight: the problem gets the most words, the proposal is often one
  sentence, and the reason is one line from their own use ("I need it to port my addon")
- Ask for the design, not spec it: no edge cases, fallbacks or "if there are unsaved changes" clauses

What gives a model away:

- Every part the same length, and every sentence complete and balanced
- A general claim in place of the writer's own case: "Event sheets and layouts are where most of the work is"
- A closing one-liner or summary: "so this message is the only lead", "X is the part no tool can check"
- Tidy groups of three, asides such as "agents included", a reply to a point that adds nothing
- Marketing words: "a huge leap forward", "unlocks", "seamless"
